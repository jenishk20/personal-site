The second offer on this site, after <a href="/three-rounds-at-adobe/">Adobe</a>. The two are connected: Adobe is the reason this loop nearly did not work, and the way it was handled is the reason it did.

Before both rounds I asked the recruiter what format to expect: resume, live coding, or behavioural for the first, and whether the second would be pair programming. Both answers changed how I prepared. Ten months later at <a href="/two-rounds-at-bloomberg/">Bloomberg</a> I did not ask the one question that would have mattered, which was whether the interviewers could read C++.

## Round 2 was not a LeetCode problem

A hash map, designed at the class level, with the tests written before the code they test. Ninety minutes, two engineers. No trick, no pattern to recognise. It is the round in my log that looks most like the actual job.

## The question I held back

Between the two rounds I got the Adobe offer: a summer internship ending on Friday 15 August. Dell's co-op would have overlapped it. The only way to do both was to start at Dell on Monday the 18th, which meant asking a team that had not yet decided whether it wanted me to change its plans for me.

I wrote to the recruiter before the final round and asked whether the team could be flexible. Her advice was to hold the question. Teams sometimes adjust the term for a candidate they really want, she said, so the move was to do the final interview first, let them decide I was their top choice, and only then raise the dates. Raised before the interview, the same request might have coloured how they saw me in it.

So I did the round without it hanging over the conversation, the offer came, and then the dates were discussed. The team agreed. I finished at Adobe on the Friday and the co-op ran from August to December.

The lesson is not specific to co-ops. A constraint is a negotiation, and a negotiation goes better once the other side has already decided it wants you. The same request lands differently before and after that point.

It is also worth saying that this was a recruiter actively on my side. Set it against the <a href="/four-rounds-at-amazon-seattle-hq/">Amazon</a> loop, where the recruiter stopped replying, or Bloomberg, where my feedback never got an answer.

## What was not negotiable

Two things I asked about after the offer, both with clear answers. Co-ops get no relocation or travel support, so the hour each way was mine to solve. And when I asked whether the hourly rate could reflect my earlier years as an engineer, the answer was that co-op rates are fixed, and that the time to negotiate is a full-time offer.

Neither answer was a surprise, and asking cost nothing. Not asking is the only way to be sure the answer is no.
