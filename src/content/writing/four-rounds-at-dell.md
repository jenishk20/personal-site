## The design round

Rounds 2 and 3 were one 90 minute session with two engineers. The first half was a LeetCode easy, a sliding window. The second was a hash map, designed at the class level, with the tests written before the code they test. No trick, no pattern to recognise. It is the round in my log that looks most like the actual job.

## The question I held back

I already had a summer internship lined up: Adobe, accepted the December before, ending on Friday 15 August. Dell's internship would have overlapped it. The only way to do both was to start at Dell on Monday the 18th, which meant asking a team that had not yet decided whether it wanted me to change its plans for me.

I wrote to the recruiter before the final round and asked whether the team could be flexible. Her advice was to hold the question. Teams sometimes adjust the term for a candidate they really want, she said, so the move was to do the final interview first, let them decide I was their top choice, and only then raise the dates. Raised before the interview, the same request might have coloured how they saw me in it.

So I did the final session without it hanging over the conversation, the offer came, and then the dates were discussed. The team agreed. I finished at Adobe on the Friday and the Dell internship ran from August to December.

It is also worth saying that this was a recruiter actively on my side. Set it against the <a href="/four-rounds-at-amazon-seattle-hq/">Amazon</a> loop, where the recruiter stopped replying, or <a href="/two-rounds-at-bloomberg/">Bloomberg</a>, where my feedback never got an answer.
